## The function it fulfills

Packaging might bring to mind, first and foremost, protecting the goods inside from damage. And that's true. Its job also includes protecting against spoilage or loss of quality. Well-chosen packaging makes storage easier, allows for safe transport, reduces costs at various stages of sale, and provides convenience for the end customer.

But the role packaging plays goes much further. A well-designed label makes it possible to quickly inform the customer about what the product contains, how it should be used, what ingredients it's made of, or what sets it apart. By delivering that information and catching the eye, packaging has a clear influence on the willingness to buy a given product.

But that's not all. Packaging complements the four elements of the so-called marketing mix. It plays a part in promoting a brand, raises the prestige of a product, and suggests that the solution we're offering is more innovative and interesting than the competition's. Unassuming as it may seem, packaging plays a big role in how free markets function, because studies show that an attractively packaged product sells more often. So if we want a successful sale, it's worth paying attention to the packaging we use.

## The psychological effect

Why does packaging have such enormous power? Mainly because it appeals to sight, which for most of us plays the biggest role in how we perceive things. How does that translate into better sales results?

Beyond catching the audience's attention, packaging also creates a sense of uniqueness even where there's little of it to be found. This holds true not just in big-box stores, where the goods on offer consist of dozens of nearly identical options, but really anywhere the daily fight for customers is happening.

Of course, customers themselves also expect attractive — or, put another way, original and fashionable — packaging. Buyers, more or less consciously, are drawn to options that stand out aesthetically, and they pay not necessarily for what they need, but for what they like. Well-designed packaging is a more effective form of advertising than demonstrations, contests, free samples, or even the best-arranged store display. It's no accident that its role is described as "the silent salesman."

## Good design — what does it look like?

For packaging to fulfill all its functions, its form and visual layer need to be carefully designed. Almost every detail matters, because it's the details that determine whether a campaign succeeds. Packaging has to be durable and easy to store. A carefully developed design, based on high-quality materials that combine sustainability with strength, guarantees those qualities.

The process of creating the graphic side of packaging should also be left to professionals. What matters is the shape, the type of composition used, the size and style of the lettering, the colors used and the message they carry, the design's style, and any symbols used. The label and its contents — the product name, tagline, or company logo — matter enormously, of course. Every element of the packaging should work together with the others, so that the whole composition is coherent, interesting, and innovative.

## What's worth remembering?

On the other hand, it's worth remembering that packaging doesn't come in just one form. Although in most cases we picture a box hiding a product inside, packaging can take many different shapes. To grab customers' attention, ever more interesting and surprising options are being used.
