The first step is **choosing the bag to print on**. Before doing anything else, it's worth choosing the bag you'll use for the project — its size, handle type, and the material it's made from. The next step is designing the print for the bag. If that poses any difficulty, it's better to get help from professionals. The last step is the actual printing on the bag. **How the print is applied depends mainly on the print run.** In practice, it looks like this:

- for larger runs, flexography works well, since it allows printing across the entire surface of the bag,
- for medium-sized runs, offset printing works well — it lets the print be finished with matte or glossy laminate film, hot foil stamping, or even UV varnish applied over matte laminate, and in this version the print can also be raised,
- for the smallest orders, screen printing works well.

So what printing methods can we distinguish?

## Printing methods!

One of the more popular methods for printing on [paper bags](/en/custom-logo-paper-bags) or fabric bags is screen printing. In this process, a special mesh is used to help apply the graphic onto the bag's surface. It works by **transferring an image onto paper or another material**, placed on a special fabric with a screen-like texture. The screen is pressed against the surface, and a squeegee passes over it, pushing ink through the openings in the mesh. After that step, the screen can be lifted and the quality of the resulting image checked. **The goal of this method isn't photographic accuracy** — it's more about a graphic whose value comes from unpredictable effects, such as varying color saturation, dots, or clearly defined outlines.
